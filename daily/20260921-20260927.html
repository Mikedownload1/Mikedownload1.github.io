[arXiv260921] Cloud-Side Transactional Orchestration Framework for Resource-Constrained Embedded Systems

arXiv
sysembedded systems and IoT commerceTransactional Backend-for-Front-End (T-BFF), Double-Handshake protocol, non-volatile flash memory, microservice orchestration, state machine migration, heap fragmentation reduction, ARM Cortex-A53
Authors
Pravin Nagare, Aditya Sabbineni, Preetam Dedu, Willison Lopes
Institution
Binghamton University, Independent Researcher, IEEE

The paper proposes a cloud-side Transactional Backend-for-Front-End (T-BFF) orchestration framework that migrates transactional state machines and microservice orchestration off resource-constrained embedded devices to reduce client-side overhead. It introduces a Double-Handshake protocol using non-volatile flash memory for state recovery after reboots. Experiments on an ARM Cortex-A53 platform show a 35% reduction in maximum heap usage and 40% improvement in end-to-end transaction latency, offering a scalable blueprint for transactional integrity on legacy IoT hardware.

[arXiv260921] SensorWF: A FAIR Generalizable Workflow Framework for Scientific Time-Series Analysis

arXiv
sysscientific workflowsFAIR principles, domain adapter pattern, PROV-O, ProvONE, SSN/SOSA, OWL ontologies, SHA-256 checksums, component registry, anomaly detection, time-series analysis
Authors
Logan Luna, Joseph Rigo, Kellan Shew, Raul Alejandro Vargas-Acosta
Institution
Georgia Institute of Technology, Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University

The paper presents SensorWF, a FAIR-annotated, five-module workflow framework for generalizable scientific time-series analysis, where domain-specific logic is isolated in a single M1 adapter so that modules M2–M5 operate identically across fields. It generates runtime provenance traces and SSN/SOSA-aligned ontologies, and is instantiated in spacecraft telemetry, ambulatory ECG, and atmospheric climate domains using only 170–500 line adapters. Results show a single codebase can support heterogeneous analytical pipelines across sensor domains without modifying the analytical core.

[arXiv260921] AI-Driven Scientific Computing Workflows: A Systems Review of Orchestration, Execution, Reproducibility and Provenance

arXiv

Authors
Jamie J. Alnasir
Institution
Royal Holloway University of London

This systems review integrates previously separate strands of research on scientific workflow management, machine-learning pipelines, AI-coupled HPC workflows and automated research workflows, proposing a continuum that describes the depth of AI participation from a single computational stage to co-adaptive workflow control. It organizes associated systems requirements around five concerns—control and orchestration, compute and execution, data and model state, reproducibility and provenance, and governance and assurance—and evaluates workflows by scientific progress, execution cost, resilience and decision traceability. The paper concludes by identifying open problems in dynamic workflow representation, state-aware recovery, heterogeneous scheduling, interoperable data planes, model-mediated decision provenance and reproducible adaptive execution.

Authors

The paper proposes Brain API, an intent-aware control plane for policy-governed agentic systems whose central contribution is the decision artifact—a durable, versioned, auditable record of how an intent became an executable plan, including which policies applied and which alternatives were rejected. The authors evaluate a prototype decision layer against two external policy corpora, achieving full agreement (42/42) with OPA Gatekeeper verdicts while Cedar-based differential testing exposed three defects, including a default-allow assumption that would invert authorization policies. Authors

Institution

This paper decomposes predictive Kubernetes autoscaling for LLM serving under long replica startup delays into token-aware demand tracking, startup-delay lookahead, uncertainty margin, and plant-state observation, evaluating each factor on production-derived heavy-tailed workloads. Authors
Herlock Rahimi, Dionysis Kalogerias
Institution

The paper characterizes the objective that distributionally agnostic FedAvg optimizes under unknown and skewed client participation, showing uniform aggregation minimizes a participation-weighted stochastic objective at an O(1/√T) rate. Authors

Institution
Independent researchers (no institutional affiliation; personal emails)

The paper evaluates LLM-generated GPU kernels from five model configurations on KernelBench level 1, finding a frontier model achieves 91.1% correctness and verified speedups on 22 of 56 problems, while open-weights models lag far behind. It then profiles seven real workloads and shows the addressable runtime fraction ranges from 8.9% to 58.2%, meaning transformer end-to-end gains are bounded near 1% (with the fraction shrinking at larger scale), whereas recommenders reach 58.2% with a projected 8.63% end-to-end improvement via the new DLRM-Bench. The authors also expose a flaw in KernelBench's absolute-tolerance correctness check (zeros pass 4 of 60 problems, including an exploited 283x kernel) and propose scale-invariant replacements.
